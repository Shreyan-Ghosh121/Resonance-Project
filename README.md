Project: Analysis of Resonance in Periodic Frequencies
1. Aim
To demonstrate the phenomenon of resonance by simulating the response of a system to periodic driving frequencies and observing how varying damping ratios affect the peak amplitude.

2. Introduction
Resonance occurs when a system is subjected to a periodic force whose frequency is equal or close to the natural frequency of the system. At this point, the system oscillates with maximum amplitude.

3. Mathematical FormulaThe relationship between the magnification factor (Amplitude A) and the frequency ratio (r) is given by:
                 A= 1/sqrt((1-r^2)^2+(2ζr)^2)
   where,
   r=Ratio of driving frequency to natural frequency
   ζ=Damping ratio (represents energy loss in the system).
   resonance occurs when r≈1.

4. Observations from SimulationResonance Peak:
   a) The amplitude reaches its maximum value when the frequency ratio r is approximately 1.
   b) Effect of Damping:
     i) Low Damping (ζ=0.1): Produces a sharp, high-amplitude peak.
     ii) High Damping (ζ=0.5): Produces a flatter, broader peak, reducing the intensity of the resonance.

5. Conclusion
The project successfully simulates periodic frequency response. It proves that resonance is highly sensitive to the match between driving and natural frequencies. In engineering, understanding these "Periodic Frequencies" is vital to ensure structures like bridges or circuits do not fail due to excessive amplitude spikes.
